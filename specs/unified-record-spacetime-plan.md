# From Σ to physical spacetime: the scientific contract (M0)

**Purpose.** The author's programme "From `Σ` to physical spacetime" (milestones M0–M9, gates A, B
and C; BACKLOG #349 onwards) asks for one ontic arena `Σ_*` — a measure, a symplectic form and a
Hamiltonian flow — in which the same dynamics writes records, gives outcome statistics, carries
clocks and signals, and supports an operational reconstruction of Lorentzian geometry with explicit
errors. This note is milestone M0. It fixes, before any further Lean is written, what the programme
**assumes**, what the corpus has **derived**, and what is **owed**, so that no later theorem can turn
a premise into a result by being stated carefully.

**Baseline.** Commit `d06f8bd7` (M1A landed: `RecordLayer/DiracPointerHamiltonian.lean`, BACKLOG
~~#349~~). Sources audited: [`POSITS.md`](POSITS.md), [`records-to-spacetime-status.md`](records-to-spacetime-status.md),
[`beyond-1p1-scoping.md`](beyond-1p1-scoping.md), [`residues.tsv`](residues.tsv). What the audit found
in them is §7.

**How to read the three registers.**

* **Register 1 (`UA-n`)** — assumed physical structure. An entry here is an input. Nothing in it is a
  result, however natural it looks, and each entry says what would discharge or constrain it.
* **Register 2 (`UD-n`)** — derived properties: Lean theorems, by name and module, each marked
  *exact* or *bound*, and marked with the **arena it is proved on**. A theorem proved on another
  arena is infrastructure for the programme; it is not a property of `Σ_*`.
* **Register 3 (`UO-n`)** — obligations whose truth the assumptions do not guarantee. Each carries a
  **repackaging test**: the condition under which a theorem with that title would only restate an
  entry of Register 1.

The identifiers `UA-`, `UD-`, `UO-` are local to this note. Posit numbers are those of
[`POSITS.md`](POSITS.md) and residue identifiers those of [`residues.tsv`](residues.tsv); neither is
renumbered or extended here.

## 1. The two distinctions the programme turns on

1. **Operational reconstruction is not derivation.** The carrier is the lattice Dirac chain, chosen
   because it has a relativistic continuum limit (UA-8). Recovering Lorentz coordinates from records
   of that carrier shows that observers inside the model reconstruct the geometry the model was
   chosen to have, with stated errors. It does not derive relativity from weaker principles. Which
   assumptions *force* the geometry is milestone M8 (gate C), and nothing before M8 may be worded as
   if it answered that.
2. **One arena, not a collection of witnesses.** The corpus proves records, single clicks, clocks,
   cone bounds and radar kinematics on several different arenas (§4.2). The programme's claims are
   about one shared arena. A result on a separate arena counts toward the programme only after a
   theorem identifies its dynamics with the flow of `Σ_*`, or re-proves it there.

A third rule follows from the corpus's own discipline and is applied throughout: **a property that
Lean proves about a definition is a property of the definition.** It becomes a property of the
dynamics only when the object it is about is produced by the flow.

## 2. The shared arena as it stands

`Σ_* = ℂℙ^{2M+1} × T²`, in Lean `KSigma (2 * M + 1 + 1)` with the form `arenaForm`
(`arenaForm_isSymplectic`, `LF4/ArenaSymplectic.lean`) and the measure `kMuL` (the Fubini–Study
measure on the base times Haar measure on the fibre, `LF4/KahlerInstance.lean`).

The joint energy is `diracPointerEnergy`, an instance of `coupledEnergy`
(`LF4/ArenaCoupledFlow.lean`):

  `𝓗(p, θ) = −2 ⟨H_D⟩_p − 2 g f(θ₂) ⟨P_j⟩_p`,

with `H_D = diracHam J μ` the one-particle Dirac ring of `M + 1` sites, `P_j = siteProj j` the
projector on site `j`, `g` a coupling constant and `f` a smooth periodic profile. Its Hamiltonian
flow `diracPointerFlow` is global and has a closed form (`diracPointerFlow_apply`): `θ₂` is constant,
the carrier is moved by the unitary `diracCarrierU` generated by `H_D + g f(θ₂) P_j`, and `θ₁` is
translated by `diracPointerShift`, whose rate is `−2 g f'(θ₂) ⟨P_j⟩`.

What this arena does **not** contain: a readout region, a ready state, a threshold, an
`ActualOccurrence`, a clock, a second observer, a second detector, an outcome probability, a causal
relation, a coordinate. The moving angle `θ₁` does not act back on the carrier: `𝓗` is independent of
`θ₁`, so the carrier sees the static potential `g f(θ₂) P_j`.

## 3. Register 1 — assumed physical structure

Each entry: what is assumed, where it enters the Lean, its standing (posit, boundary, choice), and
what would discharge or constrain it.

### UA-1 — the arena

* **Assumed.** `Σ_*` is a projective base times a two-torus fibre with the product symplectic form.
  The fibre has one moving angle `θ₁` and its partner `θ₂`.
* **Where.** `KSigma`, `arenaForm`, `arenaForm_isSymplectic`.
* **Standing.** Choice. `Σ` is the floor of the account: it is not derived and deriving it is not an
  obligation of this programme. What the programme may do is *constrain* it — show which features of
  the arena the records, statistics and geometry require (M8).
* **Linked posit.** Posit 10 (which fibre coordinates carry record content). The author's decision is
  I3 (both); the corpus computes with I1 (`torusCell`, `globalBasin` read `θ₁` only); I3 has a rate
  field from the base since BACKLOG ~~#136~~ (`jointMomentContext`,
  `skewBasin_prob_jointMomentContext`), with its degeneracy map a posit. On `Σ_*` as built the flow
  moves `θ₁` and conserves `θ₂`, which sets the coupling: the only coordinate this dynamics writes
  is `θ₁`. An M2 or M3 statement must say which coordinates its regions read.
* **Discharge.** None sought for the arena. For the record dimension, Posit 10's line: derive from
  the interaction which coordinates are written.

### UA-2 — the measure

* **Assumed.** The typicality measure is `μ_FS` on the base (Posit 2, residue R-012) and Haar on the
  fibre, in product form (Posit 9): a preparation `ψ` is the measure `δ_[ψ] ⊗ Haar`
  (`epistemicMeasure`, `RecordLayer/GlobalBasin.lean`).
* **What backs it short of derivation.** `fsMeasure_unique`: `μ_FS` is the only `U(N)`-invariant
  probability measure on the base. `epistemicMeasure_eq_disintegration` and `csd_equivariance`: the
  product form is the disintegration of the ontic measure and is carried by the unitary flow.
* **What it is not.** It is not selected by the dynamics. `flow_admits_invariant_ne_fubiniStudy`: a
  projective unitary flow fixing two distinct rays has an invariant probability measure other than
  `μ_FS`. Invariance of `kMuL` under `diracPointerFlow` (UD-5) is necessary for the measure to be
  usable and says nothing about why it is this one. Expected, not checked: the carrier generator at
  fixed `θ₂` is Hermitian (`diracPointerH_isHermitian`), hence has two distinct eigenrays, so the
  hypothesis of that theorem holds on `Σ_*`.
* **Fibre law.** `θ₂` is conserved by the flow (`diracPointerFlow_snd_snd`). No law for `θ₂` is
  produced by this dynamics: whatever distribution a theorem gives `θ₂` is preparation input (UA-4).
  Relaxation of a fibre distribution is residue R-019. The corpus has it on a hyperbolic witness
  only (`relaxation_requires_hyperbolic_fibre`), and translation flows admit no decay envelope
  (`not_hasCorrelationDecay_kFlow`). The fibre action on `Σ_*` is a translation of `θ₁` at a rate
  depending on `θ₂` and the carrier, not a hyperbolic map, so those relaxation theorems do not
  apply to it. Whether that shear relaxes the `θ₁` marginal has not been examined.
* **Discharge.** Posit 2's and Posit 9's lines in `POSITS.md`.

### UA-3 — the typicality interpretation

* **Assumed.** Probability *is* the volume ratio of the ontic measure (Posit 4). Every quantity the
  programme will call `P_{Σ_*}(i | ψ, M)` is the UA-2 measure of a region, read through this entry.
* **What backs it.** `born_frequency_convergence_N` is a coherence check (long-run frequencies under
  the product measure converge to the volume ratio). Nothing discharges the reading itself.
* **Discharge.** None known; `POSITS.md` Posit 4 records that.

### UA-4 — apparatus preparation

* **Assumed.**
  1. *The ready state.* The pointer starts at a ready value and is renewed for each measurement
     (Posit 5: `calibratedBank`, one measurement, one bank).
  2. *The apparatus parameters.* The detector's site `j`, the profile `f`, the coupling `g`, and (from
     M2) the readout region and threshold. They are the measurement context `M`. Since BACKLOG
     ~~#352~~ the regions are `diracReady` (pointer angle in `(0, 1/6]`) and `diracDisplayed`
     (pointer angle in `(1/3, 5/6]`), and the readout time `T` is a parameter of the same kind.
  3. *The law of the conserved angle.* The distribution of `θ₂` over trials.
  4. *Measurement independence* (Posit 8): the choice of `M` is not correlated with the ontic point.
     Settings are not represented in `Σ`, and nothing backs this entry.
* **What constrains it.** `collapse_accuracy_bound`: approximate collapse is paid for in the measure
  of the ready region, and exact collapse forces a null ready set. `swap_not_blockLuders`: no fixed
  ray-level calibration serves degenerate measurements. `no_joint_hilbert_map`: no map on a joint
  Hilbert space reproduces a joint lift (the record-layer scope of Wigner–Araki–Yanase).
* **A tension to keep visible.** A ready pointer is a concentrated law for `θ₁`; Posit 9's fibre
  factor is uniform. Both are in the corpus, in different modules. A statement on `Σ_*` must say
  which fibre law it uses for `θ₁` and which for `θ₂`, and may not switch between a proof's steps.
* **Discharge.** Posit 5's line (the same `H_int` family as Posit 1). For item 3, a dynamics that
  moves `θ₂`, which the `coupledEnergy` form excludes.

### UA-5 — the microscopic coupling

* **Assumed.** The interaction is the product term `−2 g f(θ₂) ⟨P_j⟩`: a base observable diagonal in
  the site basis times a function of the fibre. Its form, its locality (one site), its strength and
  its profile are chosen.
* **Standing.** Boundary R-015 (which `H_int` a given apparatus realises is a modelling input) for the
  choice. Residue R-016 concerns the arena-level generation of the shear and joint-lift propagators
  and not this one: `diracPointerFlow` is defined as the Hamiltonian flow of the energy, and
  `hamiltonianFlow_coupledEnergy` computes it.
* **What the coupling gives and does not give.** It gives a deterministic drift of the pointer
  proportional to the site occupancy (UD-4, UD-7). It gives no rate field, no outcome cell and no
  probability. The corpus's rate field is Posit 1 (UA-6).
* **Discharge.** None for the choice (a boundary). M8 compares alternatives.

### UA-6 — the cell law (Posit 1)

* **Assumed, wherever outcome cells sized by rates are used.** The rates a context assigns generate
  the phase rotations of its pointer torus: `IsTorusGenerated c`
  (`RecordLayer/CellLawForced.lean`). The corpus's field `momentContext` is a definition.
* **Standing.** Posit. §6 states exactly why it stays one.
* **Discharge.** Derive `IsTorusGenerated` from the dynamics that writes the record. Not done.

### UA-7 — the external flow parameter

* **Assumed.** The parameter `t` of `diracPointerFlow` is the ontic time. It is not an observable of
  the model and no record reads it.
* **Where it already does work.** Every existing write window, retention window, clock reading and
  radar time in the corpus is stated in `t`. The author's decision on ~~#290~~ — the ontic time order
  decides which wing of an entangled pair is selected first — makes `t` physically significant for
  individual outcomes (`spacelike_boost_changes_outcomes`), never for statistics.
* **Discharge.** Not sought (M4 allows `t` to remain fundamental). What M4 owes is that *operational*
  time is a function of clock records only (UO-14).

### UA-8 — the microscopic propagation law

* **Assumed.** The carrier is one particle on a finite ring with the lattice Dirac Hamiltonian
  `diracHam J μ` (`CV/DiracClockChain.lean`): nearest-neighbour (`diracHam_apply_eq_zero`), Hermitian
  (`diracHam_isHermitian`), `ħ = 1`. Its evolution is unitary because the base energy is the
  expectation `−2 ⟨H⟩` — the unitary class, Posit 6 (`projectedFlow_eq_unitary_family` takes it as a
  hypothesis; `projectedFlow_unitary_of_flow_continuous` reduces it to continuity).
* **Relativity is input.** The Dirac chain is chosen with a relativistic continuum limit
  (`dirac_massless_limit`, `dirac_packet_limit`). Lorentz kinematics recovered from its records is
  therefore the first distinction of §1, not a derivation.
* **Discharge.** Posit 6's line for unitarity. For the choice of carrier, M8.

### UA-9 — composite structure and locality

* **Assumed.** Local tomography for composites (Posit 7, boundary R-017:
  `compositeAlgReconstruction`, `recordLocallyTomographic_iff_adjoin_eq_top`); locality of
  de-isolation is built in (`localDeisolation_factorises` holds by construction).
* **Where it will enter.** M3's no-signalling and sequential-update checks, and M5's interaction
  topology. `Σ_*` as built is a one-particle arena with one detector; no composite is on it.

### UA-10 — stated detector laws and input weights on the existing arenas

* **Assumed in the results of §4.2.** The click law's rate `κ` and its arcs (~~#266~~), the detector
  gain (~~#252~~), and the use of the packet's site weights to size the arcs: "QM's weights are input,
  never constructed" (`records-to-spacetime-status.md`). Only ~~#251~~'s detectors are generated by a
  coupling.
* **Consequence.** `dirac_packet_one_click` is a theorem about a stated click law. Citing it on `Σ_*`
  without generating that law from `diracPointerFlow` would import the weights.

### UA-11 — kinematic idealisations in the radar results

* **Assumed in `radar_lorentz_exact`, `radar_lorentz_scaling` and the `d + 1` chart
  (`trilateration_chart_boostD`).** The messenger travels at exactly `1`; a moving clock reads
  exactly `√(1 − v²)` times rest time; the second observer's messenger is isotropic; clocks are
  calibrated and synchronised by hypothesis (`RecordLayer/RecordOccurrence.lean`). The chain's
  deviations enter as reading errors supplied to the statement, and the family along which every
  error shrinks is not instantiated on an arena.

### M3 — outcome statistics, or the obstruction (`DiracPointerBorn.lean`; gate A)

The question is the programme's: can the Hamiltonian and the apparatus determine the measures of the
outcome regions without stipulating the moment-map rate field or importing the Born weights as the
detector law?

* **UO-7 — outcome regions fixed by the context.** Regions of `Σ_*` defined from the apparatus
  parameters (UA-4) and the flow alone. *Test:* see §6 — a region built from `momentContext`,
  `circleCell` sized by `momentMap`, or arcs sized by site weights has the conclusion in its
  definition.
* **UO-8 — the error bound.** `|P_{Σ_*}(i | ψ, M) − ⟨ψ, P_i ψ⟩| ≤ ε_meas`, with every contribution to
  `ε_meas` an explicit term: transition region (UO-3), finite coupling time, back-reaction on the
  carrier, finite retention. *Not guaranteed.* See the two calculations below.
* **UO-9 — or the obstruction.** If the rate field cannot be derived: the precise missing condition,
  or a no-go theorem. Not a new hypothesis inside a definition.
* **UO-10 — one trial, one outcome.** With several detectors. *Not available on `Σ_*` as built:* the
  arena has one pointer torus and one detector site. An arena with several pointers, or one pointer
  with a partitioned readout, is an extension of M1 that has not been made.
* **UO-11 — repeated trials.** Convergence of frequencies. *Test:* a law of large numbers under the
  product of preparation measures restates UA-2, UA-3 and UA-4 (it is UD-22's
  `born_frequency_convergence_N` again). It is a result about the dynamics only if the sequence of
  trials is generated by one flow with a renewed apparatus.
* **UO-12 — back-reaction and calibration visible.** The carrier's generator contains the detector
  term (UD-3). The statistics statement must be about the coupled carrier, with the disturbance as a
  term of `ε_meas`.
* **UO-13 — no-signalling and sequential update.** On a shared arena carrying two detectors. *Test:*
  citing UD-22 is citing the sector level.

**Two calculations bearing on gate A. Neither is in Lean; both are to be proved or corrected, not
cited.**

1. *A base-independent fibre region cannot see the state.* At a fixed base trajectory the flow acts
   on the fibre by `(θ₁, θ₂) ↦ (θ₁ + s(θ₂), θ₂)` (UD-2), which preserves Haar measure on `T²`. So
   under the product preparation `δ_[ψ] ⊗ Haar` (UA-2), the probability that the fibre point lies in
   any fixed region of `T²` after any time is that region's Haar measure, whatever `ψ` is. Outcome
   statistics that depend on `ψ` therefore need one of two things: a region that depends on the base
   point (which is what `globalBasin` does, through the rate field of UA-6), or a non-uniform law for
   `θ₁` (a ready state, UA-4).
2. *A threshold on a ready pointer does not give a linear law.* With `θ₁` ready and `θ₂` uniform,
   the pointer's displacement is `f'(θ₂)` times a quantity that grows with the occupancy. In the
   idealised case where that quantity is `2 g w T` for a weight `w`, the click probability is the
   Haar measure of `{θ₂ : |f'(θ₂)| ≥ τ / (2 g w T)}`. Since `f'` is bounded, this is zero for all
   `w` below a cut-off, and it is linear in `w` only if `|f'|` has an unbounded tail, which no smooth
   periodic profile has. Choosing `f` to shape the law would place the target in the apparatus.

If these survive formalisation, the direct route from `Σ_*`'s threshold write to Born weights is
closed, and gate A's answer is an obstruction with a stated missing condition: the base-dependent
cell law (UA-6), or a different pointer dynamics.

## 6. When a theorem only repackages a posit

**The general test.** For a proposed theorem `T` about `Σ_*`, list the hypotheses and unfold the
definitions in its statement. `T` repackages a Register 1 entry if

1. a hypothesis is that entry, or is equivalent to it by a theorem already in the corpus; or
2. a definition in the statement contains the conclusion (a region sized by the weight it is then
   shown to have; a clock defined to read the time it is then shown to read); or
3. the object `T` is about is not produced by `diracPointerFlow` or an extension of it, so the
   dynamics is unused; or
4. `T` holds for every flow preserving the measure, so the Hamiltonian is unused.

A theorem that fails the test may still be worth having. It is then recorded in Register 2 as
*conditional on* the entry, with the entry named in its docstring, and it does not pass a gate.

**`IsTorusGenerated`.** The definition (`RecordLayer/CellLawForced.lean`):

```lean
def IsTorusGenerated (c : ContextField N) : Prop := IsGeneratedRateField c.rate
```

Its standing, stated once so that no milestone has to re-derive it:

* It is a **premise**. It is Posit 1 in its live form. No theorem in the corpus derives it from a
  dynamics.
* It is **extensionally equivalent to its conclusion**: `torusGenerated_eq_momentMap` gives
  `c.rate p i = momentMap p i` from it, and `isTorusGenerated_momentContext` gives it for the moment
  map. `POSITS.md` calls the pair a characterisation, not a discharge, and that is the status here.
* It holds of `momentContext` because `momentContext` is **defined** as the moment map. That is a
  fact about a definition (the third rule of §1).
* The one theorem with `IsTorusGenerated` as its conclusion, `isTorusGenerated_of_calibrated`, has as
  its hypothesis calibration against every einselected coupling, and `isCalibrated_forall_iff` shows
  that hypothesis is **equivalent to** `rate = momentMap`. It is a premise conversion (BACKLOG
  ~~#152~~): it moves the posit from the rates to the readout's calibration. It does not remove it.
  Whether calibration becomes Posit 1's live wording is the author's decision and is not taken here.
* What the M1 dynamics does supply is one half of that picture and only that half: the pointer's
  drift is the coupling's occupancy (UD-4, UD-7; the general statement is `drift_eq_sum_momentMap`).
  A drift is a mean motion of one pointer. It is not a rate field, a cell, or a probability.

**Consequences for the milestones.** Until UO-20 is met, each of the following is a restatement of
Posit 1 and may not be reported as dynamical:

* an M3 theorem with `IsTorusGenerated c` or `∀ S, IsCalibrated c.rate S` among its hypotheses;
* an M3 theorem whose outcome regions are `globalBasin (momentContext _) i`, `circleCell` sized by
  `momentMap`, or the click arcs of UD-11 sized by site weights (`globalBasin_prob` returns whatever
  rate the field supplies; `globalBasin_born` is that with the moment map put in);
* a statement that the dynamics "generates the torus action" on the strength of
  `fundamentalForm_torusField` alone — that theorem is about the back-reaction of an einselected
  coupling, at the linear level, and on `Σ_*` the carrier also runs `H_D`, which does not commute
  with `P_j`.

**The other standing cases.**

| A theorem saying… | …repackages | unless |
|---|---|---|
| the carrier evolves unitarily on `Σ_*` | UA-8 (the energy is `−2 ⟨H⟩`) | never: it is the choice of energy |
| the measure is the right one because the flow preserves it | UA-2 | never: UD-23 |
| frequencies converge to the region's measure | UA-2, UA-3, UA-4 | the trials are generated by one flow |
| the pointer starts ready | UA-4 | a reset dynamics is on the arena |
| observers recover Lorentz kinematics | UA-8, UA-11 | messenger speed and clock rate are derived from records of the flow, with their errors (and then it is still §1's first distinction) |
| a record is written | UO-4's test | its `evolve` is `diracPointerFlow` |
| the causal order is the coupling graph | UA-5 | influence is demonstrated by a response |

## 8. Keeping the contract

1. A milestone's landing updates this note in the same commit: a `UO-n` it meets moves to Register 2
   with its theorem names and its arena; a `UO-n` it meets only conditionally stays, with the
   condition named.
2. A new assumption gets a `UA-n` **before** the theorem that uses it lands, and a posit-level one
   goes to `POSITS.md` with its discharge line.
3. Every new theorem's docstring names the `UA-n` entries it depends on when they are not visible as
   hypotheses.
4. An obstruction is a result. A proved no-go, or a named missing condition, closes its `UO-n` as
   firmly as the positive statement would.
5. Identifiers are stable: add, never renumber.
